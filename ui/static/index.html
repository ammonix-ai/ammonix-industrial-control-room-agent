Welcome to the Ammonix shift theatre

A replay of 200 simulated shifts at a waste-to-energy plant, worked by Ammonix: our harness deciding from recorded past shifts, with a local fine-tuned 9B model (Ornith) as its only language model.

The plant

Ammonix runs a simulated waste-to-energy plant: it burns waste to make electricity. Trucks deliver waste into three bunkers, which feed the furnace. The picture at the top left shows the queue, the bunkers, the furnace temperature reading and the emissions column. A supervisor's directive (the day's orders) scales the delivery contract and sets the budget.

Five rules of a shift

Delivery contracteach shift must deliver a set amount of electricity (MWh)within 15% of the contract
Emissionsnever above the limit28
Furnacetemperature never above1050 °C
Budgetnet spending within the directive's cap10,000 EUR normally · 12,000 for maximum output · 7,000 when money is tight
Bunkersthree bunkers, none overfilled120 t each

Break one and the shift fails; emissions, furnace and bunker breaches end it on the spot. A safety-first directive also adds a minimum safety margin.

One tick, one decision

A shift is 24 ticks of 15 minutes. Every tick, the Ammonix system takes exactly one action, for example routing or rejecting a truck, adjusting the furnace load, cleaning the boiler or escalating to the supervisor. The feed on the right names the layer that chose it, the action and its reason.

Try this first

Press play and watch delivered energy chase the contract pace. Filter outcome: failure and read in the Shift panel which rule broke. Scrub to a SAFETY GUARD tick and read its reason; ask why if the dialog model is online.

What is on the screen (17 items)
Shift picker and filters (top bar)
Choose any of the 200 sealed shifts, or narrow the list by directive, rare event (a grid emergency, a truck nobody ordered, or a spill into a bunker) or outcome.
Outcome chip (top bar, green or red)
SUCCESS with the shift's score, or FAILED naming the rule that broke; the Shift panel always shows the full verdict.
Supervisor directive (italic line under the top bar)
The day's orders: normal operations (balanced), maximise output, conserve budget or safety first. It scales the contract and sets the budget and safety floor.
Queue (top left of the picture)
The truck at the head of the queue, waiting to be routed, tested or rejected: its declared waste class, tonnage and lab status. A red border marks an unscheduled truck.
Bunkers B0, B1, B2 (middle of the picture)
Height is tonnes stored, out of 120; colour is how dirty the plant believes the waste is.
Furnace (below the bunkers)
The temperature reading in °C, the feed rate in t/h and the fouling bar (how dirty the boiler is). A cleaning takes the line down for 3 ticks and costs 3,000 EUR.
Emissions column (right of the picture)
This tick's emissions against the limit of 28: green, amber above 60% of the limit, red above 80%.
Delivered vs contract (left, under the picture)
Delivered energy so far against the dashed contract pace. The contract is set per shift, scaled by the directive and raised further by a grid emergency.
Budget spent (beside it)
Cumulative net cost (spending minus revenue from sold surplus) against the directive's cap; the line turns red once it crosses.
Who decided this shift (left, middle)
Share of ticks taken by each layer: learned judgment (the M1 writer with the M2 verifier), pace controller, intake gate, safety guard, landing controller, experience ladder or hold.
Shift panel (bottom left)
The per-rule verdict: delivered, budget, emissions, furnace, bunkers (and a safety row if the directive's floor was missed), the reported cause of a failure and the escalation count.
Decision feed (right)
One row per tick: the layer, the action and its payload, the logged reason and what similar recorded shifts did. Crew radio, grid-emergency and escalation banners appear inline.
Shift terminated (end of the feed)
A hard violation (emissions, furnace temperature, bunker overflow) ends the shift on the spot; the feed says so and no further ticks exist.
Ask why (bottom right)
The chat: ask about the tick on screen and it answers from that tick's recorded decision and plant state. Delivery-status questions are answered from the replay ledger without a model call; free questions need the dialog model online.
Tick (bottom bar)
One 15-minute step, numbered 0 to 23, 24 per shift; exactly one action by the Ammonix system per tick.
Play, speed, scrub (bottom bar)
Step through the ticks at slow, normal or fast, or drag the slider to any tick.
Locate in universe (bottom right)
Opens this shift's path in the 3-D Knowledge Universe explorer.
